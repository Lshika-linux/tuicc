# Design decisions

Why things are shaped the way they are, when the reasoning isn't obvious from reading the code. Each section is a stable anchor (`CLAUDE/NOTES/design-decisions.md#anchor`).

## Dismiss vs. quit {#dismiss-vs-quit}

tuicc's main loop, once started, is never expected to exit in normal use. Every handler's `should_dismiss` bit (renamed from `should_exit`) means "call `Provider.dismiss_self()` and keep looping," not "terminate." `main.py`'s `while True:` has no `break` in it anywhere — the only way out is an unhandled exception, which in practice means Ctrl+C, caught quietly at the bottom of `main.py` with a `try/finally` around the loop for cleanup. See `CLAUDE/VISION.md` section 2 for the full lifecycle model this comes from.

## Optional `Provider` methods default to a documented no-op, not `abstractmethod` {#optional-provider-methods}

`mark_self()`, `dismiss_self()`, `focus_self()`, `no_focus_next_window()`, `resolve_pid()`, and `set_floating_geometry()` on `Provider` (`providers/base.py`) all default to a no-op (or `None`) rather than being abstract. Each covers a WM capability that not every window manager has an equivalent for (marks, scratchpad-style hiding, focus stealing back, per-window no-autofocus hints, non-native pid lookup, floating geometry). A provider for a WM lacking the concept simply inherits the default and accepts the resulting degraded case — tuicc shows up in its own preview, a launcher spawn's focus briefly wobbles, a restored floating window lands at the WM's default geometry instead of its saved one — never a crash. This is deliberately different from `close_window()`, which stays `abstractmethod`: every WM worth supporting can close a window, so there's no meaningful degraded case to fall back to there.

Severity varies sharply across this group even though the mechanism (no-op default) is the same: `focus_self()`'s degraded case is severe enough to call out explicitly (see `CLAUDE/NOTES/wm-quirks.md#focus-on-map-stealing`) where `mark_self()`'s or `set_floating_geometry()`'s are minor and cosmetic.

## `no_focus_next_window` is keyed on pid, not class/app_id {#pid-vs-class-criteria}

See `CLAUDE/NOTES/wm-quirks.md#no-focus-pid-criteria` for the full tradeoff — the short version: `pid` matches exactly one process and effectively never collides again, where `class`/`app_id` would keep matching every future window of that app for the rest of the WM session.

## `pending_moves.py`: pid > app_id > any-remaining matching tiers {#pending-move-tiers}

`resolve_pending_move()` matches a spawned process back to its window in three exclusive tiers, each only considering windows not already claimed by another entry: (1) exact pid match — unambiguous, but only available when the provider exposes `Window.pid` and the process wasn't launched via a shell; (2) app_id match — distinguishes the expected app from an unrelated new window, but not from a second simultaneous instance of the *same* app_id; (3) any remaining unclaimed new window — last resort.

The tiers are exclusive, not a cascade: an entry expecting a specific pid/app_id waits for exactly that (or times out in the caller) rather than settling for a weaker signal because its own match hasn't appeared yet on this tick. Falling through early looks fine until two entries share an app_id (e.g. two `kitty` launches) and one's window is simply slower to appear — a premature fallback then hands it the wrong entry's window. This exact bug reassigned 3 of 10 windows to the wrong workspace in a burst-launch test before the exclusive-tier fix.

Same-app_id entries not colliding at the app_id tier relies on callers processing pending entries in order, each claiming its match before the next entry looks — `resolve_pending_move()` itself has no notion of which window actually appeared first in real time, it just picks the first unclaimed match available to it. Two simultaneous instances of the same app_id landing on each other's targets (rather than crashing) is an accepted tradeoff, not a bug, as long as both instances are otherwise identical.

`resolve_pending_move()` doesn't mutate `claimed` itself — the caller adds the result's id only once it commits to the match (calls `move_window_to_region`), so a per-tier scan here never leaves a side effect to undo if the caller doesn't use the result.

## Why `PID_GRACE_SECONDS` is 6.0s {#pid-grace-seconds}

Was 1.5s until i3 testing surfaced a real race: `_enrich_pids()`'s `provider.resolve_pid()` call (a full `get_tree()` round-trip plus a fresh Xlib connection per call, see `i3.py`'s `_x11_pid_for_window`) isn't guaranteed to resolve a freshly-mapped window's pid within 1.5s, and once an entry downgrades to app_id-tier matching, the pid tier is gone for good — `resolve_pending_move()`'s tiers are exclusive, so a later successful enrichment can no longer help. If the app_id fallback also doesn't match (common on i3, where a Python/Electron app's real WM_CLASS often isn't its `.desktop`-derived hint), the entry just times out unmatched at `MOVE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` with nothing left to try. Widened to 6.0s to give the slower, async i3 enrichment path a real chance to land before giving up on the more precise pid signal, while still leaving a couple of seconds of runway for the app_id fallback before the hard timeout.

## `pending_moves.process()`'s three-part return/side-effect contract {#pending-moves-process-contract}

`process()` returns `(reclaimed_focus, resolved_target_regions)` and always tries to reclaim tuicc's own focus (unless `dismissed`) before dropping a timed-out entry, even for entries that never matched — a spawn's own placement failing is a separate concern from tuicc's own fullscreen/focus, which the transient co-location problem (see `CLAUDE/NOTES/wm-quirks.md#fullscreen-drop-on-map`) already broke the moment the new window mapped. Without this, a spawn that timed out unmatched left tuicc's window stuck floating and unfocused indefinitely, with nothing left in the queue to ever call `focus_self()` on its behalf again.

`reclaimed_focus` tells `main.py` whether `focus_self()` was called this round — a real WM-focus transition, but a self-inflicted one, not the user switching to a different real context. `main.py`'s own real-focus-transition detector needs to know this to avoid misreading tuicc reclaiming its own focus as the user having gone elsewhere, which would otherwise silently reset `selected_id`/`focus_id` mid-launcher-session (concrete regression: a spawn's target silently switching workspace because an earlier spawn's `focus_self()` call happened to land between selecting a workspace and confirming — see the regression test).

`resolved_target_regions` lists the target region of every entry that matched a window this round (never the give-up-unmatched path, which has no real destination to report) so `main.py` can let `focus_id` — and the preview panel — follow a spawn/restore to wherever it actually landed. Without this, the preview panel showed nothing, indefinitely, for the rest of that tuicc session after a restore completed, until a dismiss+resummon forced a reset.

`own_region_id` is compared against each resolving entry's `target_region` to decide whether to request `force_relayout` from `focus_self()` — see `CLAUDE/NOTES/wm-quirks.md#fullscreen-suppresses-layout` for why a window landing on tuicc's own fullscreen workspace needs this. `None` (the default) never requests it, same opt-in shape as `fullscreen_only`.

## Battery percent is energy-weighted, not a plain average across packs {#battery-energy-weighted-percent}

`battery.aggregate()` computes overall percent as `sum(energy_now)/sum(energy_full)*100` when every pack reports both fields — the real combined charge, not skewed by packs of different physical capacity. Concrete case that motivated this: a two-pack machine with BAT0 at 6%/22.62Wh full and BAT1 at 15%/68.7Wh full — a plain percent average reads ~10-11%, the energy-weighted combined charge is ~13%, a real, non-trivial difference given BAT1 holds triple BAT0's capacity. Falls back to a plain percent average only when any pack is missing `energy_now`/`energy_full` (some drivers only expose `charge_*`/`capacity`).

`aggregate()`'s status is "Charging" if *any* pack is charging (a topped-off sibling reporting "Not charging" shouldn't override "the machine is charging" overall), else "Discharging" if any pack is, else the first pack's own status verbatim.

## Connectivity module: signal bars, fixed-slot windowing, action feedback {#connectivity-module-design}

`_signal_bars()` always draws all 5 segments (filled + empty), never just the filled ones — an earlier 4-asterisk scheme only ever drew the filled asterisks ("*   " for a weak signal), giving no way to tell at a glance whether that's 1-of-4 or the rest of the scale simply isn't there. Uses plain Unicode Geometric Shapes (▮/▯), not a Nerd Font icon — tuicc's established convention is symbols any reasonable monospace font already has, and a signal indicator is exactly the kind of thing that must never render as a broken tofu box. `None` (backend can't report a signal) renders as all-empty rather than a distinct glyph, keeping the "always 5 segments" consistency intact.

WiFi/Bluetooth each use the fixed-slot-plus-scroll-window mechanic (`windowed_list.py`, shared with media.py/sysmon.py) instead of an earlier hand-rolled "cap WiFi at a fixed row count with a static '+N more' line, don't cap Bluetooth at all": with no cap on Bluetooth, a long pairing history could push WiFi off-screen entirely, and "+N more" had no way to actually reach those extra networks. The section header and its Scan/Discover trigger share one row (header left-aligned, trigger right-aligned) rather than the trigger getting its own row, to give the scrollable list back one more row of height — the trigger is still its own distinct NavItem/target_kind, matching sysmon.py's CLOSE-vs-KILL idiom for "second action on a section."

The Scan/Discover trigger's "Scanning…"/"Discovering…" label is driven by main.py's dedicated `wifi_scanning`/`bluetooth_discovering` Domains (polling the daemon's own real `Scanning`/`Discovering` property), not `StatusWorker.is_pending()` — the fire-and-forget `scan()`/`start_discovery()` call itself only pends for a brief D-Bus round trip, nowhere near the real scan/discovery duration, so `is_pending()` alone made the label flicker instead of holding steady for the actual scan.

A wifi passphrase overlay's "waiting" state must stay open until the real connect result (success/failure) is known, not close the instant Enter is pressed — closing early meant a wrong passphrase produced total silence (the connect attempt kept running in the background with nothing on screen reflecting it, indistinguishable from tuicc having silently dropped the input).

An unknown wifi network shows a "[new] " *prefix*, not a " (new)" suffix — a prefix reads as a real label attached to the name ("this row is new"), where a suffix could get pushed out of view entirely by a long SSID. Signal bars get their own color independent of the row's selection/dim styling (accent when connected, plain text color otherwise, never dimmed) so signal strength stays legible to scan regardless of which row is currently selected.

`_action_progress_line()` (a hover-preview line reflecting a live connect/disconnect attempt for one specific item) exists because pressing Enter on a network/device previously gave no feedback beyond the row's own blink, and no way to tell *why* it failed. It uses `get_action_error_for()`, not the plain per-domain `get_action_error()`, because many networks/devices share one "wifi"/"bluetooth" domain — without the per-item key, an old network's stale error would bleed onto whatever's selected now. `_wifi_scan_preview_text()` similarly shows the *full* network list on hover, not just the visible-slots window the scrollable section itself shows, since the box's own row budget can't show everything at once.

## sysmon's compact stats grid: layout, formatting, and per-metric choices {#sysmon-stats-grid}

`modules/sysmon.py`'s middle stats block is a real N-column table (`GRID_COL_WIDTH` = 18, `_grid_row()`), not a loose run-on line of "LABEL value LABEL value ..." — a fixed column boundary is what actually reads as tidy and scannable, verbatim: "the config should be the one place you configure everything about that box" is why layout, per-metric visibility, and thresholds all became real `[[sysmon.block]]` config.toml entries (`config.py`'s `_build_sysmon_blocks()`/`DEFAULT_SYSMON_BLOCKS`) rather than constants in this module — see `CLAUDE/VISION.md`'s R6 section for the full config shape. Metric labels render in accent color, values in plain text color, so the eye can scan for just labels or just values instead of a flat wall of text.

Every cell except the last in a row is padded/truncated to `GRID_COL_WIDTH` so the next column lines up; the last cell keeps its natural length. This is deliberate, not an oversight: HOT's own `describe_sensor()` label (e.g. "58°C (CPU (Package id 0))") routinely runs past `GRID_COL_WIDTH`, and in the packaged default layout it's the one cell with no fixed-width neighbor to its right — a user who repositions that block into a row that *does* share width with another column takes on the truncation risk themselves, a consequence of their own layout choice, not something the function tries to prevent for them.

Per-metric formatting choices: RAM and disk show used/available amounts (e.g. "5.2/12.0 GiB"), not a bare percent — a percentage alone doesn't say how much room is actually left, though the cell's color still follows the real usage percent even though the displayed text doesn't show one. RAM uses GiB (binary, /1024², matching what the kernel's own MemTotal/MemAvailable already round to); disk uses GB (decimal, /1e9, matching `df`'s own convention) — deliberately different units for the two, not an inconsistency. The `cputemp` metric is labeled "CPUTEMP", not "TEMP", for clarity against the "HOT" (hottest sensor) block that often sits right next to it in the packaged layout — same value shape, different meaning (one is specifically the CPU package, the other is whatever sensor is hottest right now). THROTTLED rides along on HOT's own cell as a separate urgent-colored segment specifically because that's the one cell layout-wise that isn't at risk of column-width clipping in the packaged default.

`_friendly_app_name()` shows the app's display name ("Firefox"), not the raw app_id ("firefox") or the full window title — a resource-monitor row cares about *which app*, not *which document/tab*, and a full title (VS Code/Firefox tabs routinely run 40+ characters) would otherwise push the CPU/RAM numbers themselves off the edge of the box. It reuses launcher.py's `scan_desktop_apps()` cache (matching a window's app_id against a `.desktop` entry's `StartupWMClass`/filename hint, case-insensitively — looser than pending_moves.py's own app_id tier, which needs an exact match to disambiguate multiple pending spawns; a display-name lookup has no such collision risk) rather than a hardcoded app_id-to-name table, which CONTRIBUTING.md's "no hardcoded personal preferences" rule would rule out anyway. `_format_window_label()` always shows the CPU/RAM readout in full and truncates the app name instead — the numbers are the actual point of a system-monitor row, so they get priority over the name when width runs out.

## sysmon module: row-expansion model, NICE's write range, action reuse {#sysmon-module-design}

`modules/sysmon.py` (`CLAUDE/VISION.md`'s R6) uses the same two-level browsing/expanded row model `sessions.py` established: level 1 is one row per window with CLOSE/KILL/NICE dimmed at the box's right edge; Enter expands the row, lighting those three actions up (`_action_positions` reuses sessions.py's own right-aligned layout logic, 3 actions instead of 4). NICE additionally opens its own small inline numeric input ("-3_", the same in-place idiom sessions.py's rename field uses) rather than running immediately — `is_editing_nice()`/`start_nice_edit()`/`handle_nice_key()`/`apply_nice_edit()` mirror sessions.py's `is_naming()`/`start_naming()`/`handle_naming_key()`/`apply_naming()` quartet shape exactly. `_expanded_window_id`/`_nice_target`/`_nice_input` are plain module-level state, same pattern as sessions.py's `_expanded_slot`/`_naming_slot`/`_name_input` and media.py's `_expanded_bus_name`.

NICE only ever writes 0..19 (positive/"nicer" only) — lowering niceness (negative values) needs `CAP_SYS_NICE`/root tuicc doesn't assume it has, and `os.setpriority()` would just fail depending on the kernel; restricting the input range to what's always safe sidesteps that failure mode entirely rather than handling it after the fact.

CLOSE/KILL both act through pieces that already exist elsewhere: `Provider.close_window()` (already implemented on both sway.py/i3.py, no provider changes needed) and a plain `pending_confirm` dict routed through `actions.py`'s existing `spawn_detached(shell_true=False)` — KILL needed zero changes to `actions.py` itself, `kill -9 <pid>` already splits cleanly via `shlex.split`, exactly like sessions.py's own "del" action.

## NetworkManager backend: real structural differences from iwd.py {#networkmanager-vs-iwd}

`connectivity/networkmanager.py` is the second `WifiBackend`, alongside `iwd.py`'s, for users who don't run iwd. It differs from iwd.py in ways that aren't just different method names:

1. **No bulk tree walk.** iwd/bluez both implement `org.freedesktop.DBus.ObjectManager.GetManagedObjects()` at their root, fetching their whole object graph in one call. NetworkManager doesn't expose ObjectManager the same way — its own `GetAllDevices()`/`Settings.ListConnections()` are the real enumeration mechanism, and each object's properties need their own `Properties.GetAll` call. More D-Bus round trips per poll than iwd.py needs — an accepted cost, no different in spirit from iwd.py's own one-GetAll-per-network pattern, just one level less bulk.
2. **`AccessPoint` is per-BSSID, not per-SSID.** iwd's `Network` object is already collapsed to one per SSID; NetworkManager's scan results are one `AccessPoint` object per physical radio actually seen (a mesh, or multiple APs on the same SSID, show up as several objects). `merge_access_points_by_ssid()` is what iwd.py never needed.
3. **No built-in link between a scan result and a saved profile.** iwd's `Network.KnownNetwork` property points straight at it; NetworkManager's `Settings.Connection` profiles are a wholly separate object graph, correlated with a scanned `AccessPoint` only by matching decoded SSID (`find_known_profile_for_ssid()`).
4. **`ActivateConnection()`/`AddAndActivateConnection()` return almost instantly**, before real activation starts — the same "returns cleanly even on a wrong passphrase" gotcha already fixed for iwd.py's own `connect()`, but structurally worse here: unlike iwd.py's one-shot post-call property check (`Connect()` itself blocks for the whole attempt), NetworkManager's activation is genuinely asynchronous even from the calling client's perspective, needing an actual bounded poll loop (`_wait_for_activation()`), not a single read.

**Not yet confirmed against a real, running NetworkManager** (built and fixture-tested against hand-built, doc-accurate D-Bus reply shapes on a machine that runs iwd) — whether `ActivateConnection` really behaves as documented on some real distro's NM version, and whether polling `State` really observes the transitions assumed here, are both unverified. Flagged explicitly rather than presented as proven the way iwd.py's live-tested behavior is.

## `spawn_detached`'s env layering: captured values win, except live session identifiers {#spawn-detached-env-layering}

`spawn_detached(env=...)` layers a captured `/proc/<pid>/environ` snapshot (session.py's `read_environ()`) on top of a copy of tuicc's own current environment — captured values win, since the whole point is restoring app-specific setup a plain relaunch wouldn't have. Exception: `_ALWAYS_LIVE_ENV_KEYS` (`WAYLAND_DISPLAY`, `DISPLAY`, `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS`, `I3SOCK`, `SWAYSOCK`, `XAUTHORITY`) always come from the *current* environment regardless of what the snapshot says — a session saved today and loaded after a relogin/reboot would otherwise carry yesterday's now-nonexistent socket paths straight into the respawned process, a launch failure with a confusing, unrelated-looking cause.

See `CLAUDE/NOTES/known-limitations.md#restore-relaunch-crash` for the concrete bug (`spawn_detached`'s `log_path` param exists for) this env handling is paired with.

## `ActionContext.reselect_item_id`: exact id vs. region search {#reselect-item-id-vs-region-search}

`ActionContext` has two single-use "move selection here after this action" signals: `reselect_region_id` (main.py searches the current frame's nav items for a region-kind item matching this id) and `reselect_item_id` (main.py jumps directly to this exact NavItem id, no search). The direct form exists because a handler sometimes already knows precisely which id it wants — typically its own module's — where a region search doesn't apply (there's no "region" to search for, just an exact row). sessions.py's "expand a row" handler is the motivating case: expanding changes what its own `nav_items()` reports on the very next frame, making the just-selected row id vanish from the list, which would otherwise trip main.py's "selection no longer valid" recovery (a region-kind fallback search, meant for real external focus changes) into jumping to the sidebar instead of landing on the newly-revealed actions.

## Media module: collapsed rows, fixed-slot sections, transport-cluster layout {#media-module-layout}

`modules/media.py`'s now-playing rows use the same two-level browsing/expanded model `sessions.py` established: each player collapses to a single NavItem row until expanded (Enter), rather than always exposing its 1-3 transport controls as separate NavItems. This isn't just visual — three always-active transport NavItems per row, positioned near the box's right edge, interacted badly with `tab_order`'s default "columns_first" ordering (sorts by x first): a row visually near the top of the box could land navigationally near the bottom of the Tab sequence, since its x was large even though its y was small. Collapsing to one NavItem per row at the browsing level sidesteps this; the transport actions only become separate NavItems once that player is expanded.

The player list is genuinely dynamic (a player can quit, appear, or reorder between polls), so `_expanded_bus_name` tracks the expanded player by its stable D-Bus identity, not a list position, and `_reconcile_expanded_state()` clears it if that player disappears while expanded — otherwise `is_expanded()` would report `True` forever for a player that no longer exists.

Both sections (Now Playing's players, Output's sinks) always render exactly `VISIBLE_SLOTS` (3) rows — never more, never fewer — regardless of how many players/sinks actually exist. Fewer than 3 real items: remaining slots show a `"[empty - player N]"`/`"[empty - output N]"` placeholder. More than 3: a scroll window keeps whatever's selected/expanded in view, recomputed fresh from `ctx.selected_id` every call (same "derive from what's true right now" idiom `_reconcile_expanded_state` uses) so it can't drift stale. media.py was the first module to get this treatment; the underlying tension (box size is a static config ratio, content amount is a live runtime quantity) applies elsewhere too — `connectivity.py`'s wifi/bluetooth lists have the same shape — but `control.py`/`power_menu.py`/`sessions.py` don't need it since their content count is a config.toml choice, not runtime-variable.

Once anything in the Now Playing section is expanded, every row's info text (dot + label) goes dimmed — including the expanded row's own label, not just its siblings: once inside a row, its name isn't what you're navigating anymore, its transport controls are, and only the one truly-focused control glyph stays bright among those.

The transport-control cluster ("◀◀ ▶ ▶▶") uses Unicode from the same block as the ●/○ status dots already confirmed safe on the target terminal (U+25A0-25FF, "Geometric Shapes") rather than "Miscellaneous Technical" (⏮/⏸/⏭), which rendered double-width via emoji-font fallback and broke column alignment — curses' `addstr` always advances by exactly 1 column per Python character regardless of the glyph's actual drawn width. Play/pause uses a single character in both states ("▶"/"■") rather than two glyphs of different lengths, since no left/right padding split ever looks truly centered when a 1-char glyph sits next to a 2-char one — matching lengths is the actual fix, not a cleverer split.

## Media module: cava visualizer placement and source-label heuristics {#media-cava-and-source-label}

The cava frequency visualizer is drawn inline to the right of the Output section's rows (not as separate fixed rows below everything), filling the same space to the right of a sink name that "◀◀ ▶ ▶▶" fills to the right of a Now Playing row. It always spans the full `VISIBLE_SLOTS` window rather than however many real sinks exist, so the idle baseline sits on a consistent fixed bottom slot rather than shifting with sink count. The idle/no-frame baseline draws on only the bottommost row, in plain "text" theme color (not also dimmed — the glyph's own short 1/8-height fill already reads as quiet without dimming on top) rather than "accent" (which read as too visually loud next to the Output list's quieter styling).

`_source_label()` picks the "where is this playing from" tag: a browser's current domain (MPRIS's `xesam:url`) when available, else the app's `DesktopEntry` (shorter/more uniform than `Identity`, e.g. "firefox" not "Mozilla firefox"), else `Identity` as the last resort. One exception: native apps can tag their metadata with a URL pointing at their own web player (Spotify's `xesam:url` is an `open.spotify.com` track link) — that's not "you're browsing this site" the way a real browser tab's URL is, so showing `[open.spotify.com]` for the desktop app would be misleading. Detected by the app's `desktop_entry` showing up inside the domain (`desktop_entry="spotify"` in `"open.spotify.com"`); when that matches, `Identity` is preferred over both the domain and `DesktopEntry`, since `Identity` is usually the nicer-cased name ("Spotify" vs "spotify") in exactly this case.

## Bars module is separate from control, not sliders bolted onto it {#bars-separate-from-control}

`modules/bars.py` (vertical VOL/BRI/BAT gauges) is a deliberately separate module from `control.py`, not sliders added to it. `control.py`'s own visual rule is "ONE box, uniform dense rows (dot + label) — not one framed box per feature"; a continuous gauge is a different visual language, and BAT in particular isn't something you toggle at all (`CLAUDE/VISION.md`'s four-category test calls that shape "Context": read-only decision input), so it doesn't belong in control's row list either way. Inspired by swcc's (this project's predecessor) own vertical bars, rebuilt with finer fill resolution via eighth-block glyphs (`render_utils.eighth_block_level` — the same technique `modules/media.py`'s cava visualizer uses) to fix swcc's own "bars move inconsistently with the real percentage" complaint. No fixed reference ticks (25/50/75% marks) — tried, read as visual clutter, dropped. A per-bar framed box was also tried and dropped as "too much" for what's meant to be a plain, dense display; bars ended up flat, same footprint as the first cut (BAR_W columns, BAR_GAP between them, no border around the fill).

This first pass is display-only — `nav_items()` returns `[]` on purpose (same as `clock.py`). VOL/BRI grab/adjust (Enter grabs, ←→ ±5%, Enter/Esc releases) is planned as `input_claim`'s 4th consumer (see `CLAUDE/VISION.md`'s R2/R5 sections) as its own follow-up pass.

## Bars: quantized fill vs. exact readout, and why empty cells draw nothing {#bars-fill-quantization}

The readout text above each bar always shows the real, exact live percent (61%, not rounded). The bar's own visual fill is a deliberately coarser, independent number: quantized to the nearest `BAR_FILL_STEP` (5%, `_quantize_percent()`) before it reaches `eighth_block_level()`. Without this, the same real percentage change (say 59% → 61%) could look like a visible jump on one box height and nothing at all on another, purely because `eighth_block_level`'s `num_rows*8` resolution (8 is a hard Unicode constant — block glyphs only come in eighths) doesn't divide evenly into 100, and that remainder shifts with box height. Quantizing the filled value first makes the visible step boundaries (every 5%) height-independent; `num_rows*8` still governs how smoothly the already-quantized value renders within a row, not where the boundaries fall. 5% also matches the ±5%/keypress step the future VOL/BRI grab interaction will use, so the bar's visual language and how you move it agree 1:1.

A partial eighth-block glyph (▁▂▃▄▅▆▇) only paints ink across the fraction of the cell it represents. Two earlier attempts at rendering the *rest* of that cell — a "░" track texture, then a solid colored background — both ended up visibly mismatched against the empty rows around them, in two different ways. The fix that actually worked: draw nothing at all for a fully-empty row and for a partial row's unpainted remainder alike (`draw()`'s `if level == 0: continue`) — both "empty" cases render as the exact same thing (nothing, blending into the terminal's own background), trivially consistent with each other by construction, with no separate shade to mismatch in the first place.

## BAT bar's three states: Charging > plugged > plain {#bars-battery-states}

`_bat_spec()` distinguishes three mutually exclusive states, in priority order: Charging (actively adding charge — "CHG" readout, accent glow via `BarSpec.charging`) > plugged (a charger is connected per `battery.get_ac_online()`, but this pack isn't presently accepting charge — a ThinkPad charge_control threshold pausing mid-range, or already topped off) > plain (genuinely running on battery alone). Charging needs to visually pop, not just say "CHG" in text, hence the accent glow on the fill itself, not just the readout.

"plugged" shows the real percent in the same plain styling as an ordinary reading (an earlier accent-tinted version of "plugged" was tried and dropped as visually confusing next to real charging) and swaps the *label* from "BAT" to "AC" instead — the label is what actually communicates "there's a charger here" without implying an active charge. Before this distinction existed, "plugged in but paused" and "genuinely running on battery alone" looked identical (both a plain percentage), even though they're very different things to know.

`battery.get_ac_online()` is a deliberately separate signal from any pack's own `status` field: a charger can be fully connected with every pack correctly declining to charge (e.g. a ThinkPad's charge_control threshold keeping a pack between 40-80%), and without a dedicated "is a charger physically connected" check, tuicc had no way to distinguish that from "genuinely unplugged, draining" — both read as a plain percentage with no charging status. It checks *every* entry under `/sys/class/power_supply`, not a hardcoded `AC` name, because a real ThinkPad exposes charging via both a traditional `AC` (type=Mains) node and two USB-C PD ports (type=USB) simultaneously — hardcoding `AC` alone would miss USB-C-only charging setups. Returns `None` (not `False`) when the machine exposes no non-battery power sources at all, distinct from "checked, none are online" — the same None-vs-`[]` discipline used throughout this codebase (see `status_worker.py`).
